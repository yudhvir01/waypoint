# Words and Writing

## TL;DR

- Select a word or a short phrase in a note, then tap **Define** on the
  toolbar that appears. A card shows its meanings, an example and, for most
  single words, the pronunciation with a ▶ button.
- **Check writing**, above every note, finds spelling, grammar and style
  problems and fixes each one with a tap.
- Pressing Enter starts the next line plain: a colour, highlight or size
  stays on the text you gave it.
- English only. Both need an internet connection, and only send text when
  you press the button.

## Looking up a word

Select some text in any note. The formatting toolbar (bold, size, colours)
appears above it, and when the selection is a word or a phrase of up to six
words, it ends with **Define**. Tap it and the toolbar grows a card with:

- the word and, if known, how it's pronounced, with ▶ to play it
- its meanings, grouped by part of speech (noun, verb, adjective…), up to
  three each, with an example sentence where there is one
- where the definition came from

The note isn't changed. Select something else or tap outside to close it.
Commas, brackets and quotes around a selection are ignored, so selecting
"ubiquitous," finds *ubiquitous*.

## Phrases

Phrases and idioms work too: select *in lieu of*, *by and large* or
*take for granted* and Define finds them. Single words are looked up in the
Free Dictionary first, which has the pronunciations; phrases, and words it
doesn't know, come from Wiktionary.

If neither has it, the card says so. That usually means the selection is a
name, a typo, or a phrase that isn't a fixed expression, so try fewer words.

## Checking your writing

**Check writing** sits next to **Image** and **Audio** above the note. Press
it and a list opens of what could be better, each marked as one of:

- **spelling**: a word that isn't spelled right
- **grammar**: "he go" for *he goes*, "can be get" for *can be got*
- **style**: wordy, repeated or weak phrasing, the "make this sentence
  better" kind of suggestion

Each has the suggested fixes as buttons: tap one and the note changes. Tap
the problem itself to jump to it in the note, or **Ignore** to drop it from
the list. The list keeps up as you edit, so fixing something by hand removes
it too.

It checks British English, so *colour* and *organise* aren't flagged. It
suggests fixes to words and phrases; it doesn't rewrite whole sentences or
paragraphs for you.

The checker is [LanguageTool](https://languagetool.org)'s free service. The
note's text is sent to it only when you press the button, and a note can be
up to about 20,000 characters. If you check many times within a minute, it
asks you to wait a moment.

## New lines start plain

Colour a word, highlight it or make it bigger, then press Enter at the end of
it: the next line starts as plain text. The formatting stays on what you
gave it. To carry on in the same colour, pick it again on the new line.

## Turning a word into a flashcard

To remember a word, write it as a flashcard line in the note:

```
ubiquitous :: present everywhere
```

The **Flashcards** chapter explains how those are reviewed.
