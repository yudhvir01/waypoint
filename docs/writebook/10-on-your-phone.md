# On Your Phone

Waypoint works in any browser, installs like an app, and has an Android app.
This chapter covers getting it onto a phone, how it keeps itself up to date,
and the gestures.

## Installing

### Android: from the browser (recommended)

1. Open **waypoint.yudhvir.in** in Chrome.
2. Scroll to **Get the app** and tap **Install Waypoint**. If you don't see the
   button, open the **⋮ menu** and choose **Install app**.
3. Confirm. Waypoint appears on your home screen and opens full-screen.

There's no download, no security warning, and it updates itself.

### Android: the app file (APK)

1. On the same page, tap **Download the APK**, then open the file when it
   finishes.
2. If Android says it can't install from this source, tap **Settings** and
   allow installs from your browser, then go back.
3. If Play Protect warns about an unknown app, choose **Install anyway**.
4. Tap **Install**, then open Waypoint.

### iPhone and iPad

1. Open the site in **Safari** (not Chrome).
2. Tap **Share → Add to Home Screen → Add**.
3. Open it from your home screen.

### Computer

Open the site in any browser. In Chrome or Edge you can also use the install
icon in the address bar.

## Keeping it up to date

The website and the installed web app update themselves. The Android app has two
layers:

- **The app's screens and features** update quietly in the background. When you
  open the app online it checks and downloads what's new. It switches to the new
  version when you next leave the app (go to the home screen or another app), so
  it never changes under you while you're writing. To switch immediately, use
  **Settings → About → Check for updates**, then **Restart now to use it**.
- **The app itself** (rare) needs a new install file. When that happens a notice
  appears at the bottom of the screen and the download starts **by itself**,
  with a progress bar. When it finishes, Android shows its own
  "Do you want to update this app?" screen. Tap **Install**. That tap is
  Android's requirement for any app outside the Play Store; the app can't do it
  for you.

The first time, Android also asks you to allow Waypoint to install updates:
the notice has an **Open settings** button, you switch it on, and you come
back. You only do this once.

If you'd rather not update right now, tap **Not now**; it won't ask about that
version again. To check yourself, use **Settings → About → Check for
updates**. Your data stays through an update: it's the same app with the same
storage.

Before anything is installed the app checks the file against a fingerprint
published with the update and that it's really Waypoint, and Android refuses it
unless it was signed with the same key as the app you have.

## Gestures

On a phone the sidebar is a drawer:

- **Swipe right** from the left side of the screen to open it. It follows your
  finger, and settles open or shut depending on how far you went or how fast you
  flicked.
- **Swipe left** anywhere to close it, like drawing a curtain.
- The ☰ button in the corner does the same.

The swipe is ignored where it would get in the way: in text fields, inside the
note editor, and on anything that scrolls sideways, such as the activity grid.
Android's own back gesture lives at the very edge of the screen, so you can
start the swipe a little further in.

## If something doesn't work

- **"Downloaded" but nothing changed:** open **Settings → About → Update
  details**. It shows which version is running, which one is waiting, and what
  has been downloaded, and says so when an update was downloaded but didn't take
  effect. **Restart now to use it** switches to the waiting version straight
  away and shows the reason if it can't.
- **No Install button on the site:** Chrome only offers it when the app isn't
  already installed. Use the ⋮ menu.
- **The update notice says it couldn't finish:** check your connection and tap
  **Try again**. A download that doesn't match its fingerprint is deleted
  rather than installed.
- **Google sign-in opens a browser and doesn't come back:** reinstall the app.
  Android verifies the link between the site and the app when it's installed.
